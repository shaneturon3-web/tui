# Jurisdiction gate — candidate dimension

Working model:

\[
T = \Delta(C,A,K) \mid J
\]

`C` = causality; `A` = agency; `K` = knowledge; `J` = jurisdiction over who or what has the right to interpret, decide, preserve, or close a question.

**Status:** `J` is a candidate transversal dimension. Do not report it as validated or as an independent fourth variable unless independent cases support that distinction.

## Apply before KEEP or before adding explanatory prose

1. **FUNCTION** — What work does this support, person, scene, explanation, or institution perform?
2. **NECESSITY** — Is that work still needed in this piece or at this point in the book?
3. **JURISDICTION** — Should this carrier have the right to perform or interpret that work here?
4. **POSITION** — Does this work belong in this piece now, or is it reserved for another piece or later reading position?
5. **MATERIALITY** — Is this carrier causally irreplaceable, or can the function migrate, distribute, or remain implicit?
6. **DECISION** — `KEEP | DELETE | SUBSTITUTE | REDISTRIBUTE | EXPAND | COMPRESS | WITHHOLD`.

## Falsifiable test for J

Seek independent cases where `C`, `A`, and `K` remain substantially intact while the authorized interpreter/decider/preserver/closer changes. For each case, record:

- the exact question whose jurisdiction changes;
- which carrier loses, gains, or shares jurisdiction;
- whether the change alters any C/A/K assignment;
- an alternative explanation (position, authorial contract, or material necessity);
- a counterexample where a locally functional carrier retains legitimate jurisdiction.

Do not count repeated analysis of Ms-01/Ms-04 as independent recurrence. They can calibrate the test but cannot alone establish generality. If no clean cases appear, retain `J` as a useful audit question or fuse it into montage/authority constraints; do not force a fourth variable.

## Usurpation-of-authority adversarial case

A sentence or scene can be correct and still be premature or out of place. Flag it when it interprets, decides, preserves, or closes something that the current carrier/scene/position has not earned jurisdiction to settle. The fix may be relocation, redistribution, withholding, or deletion; it is not automatically simplification.
